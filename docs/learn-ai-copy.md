## Notes

**The word is consulting.** It's in the first line of the hero and again at the top of
section 3. Coaching, mentoring and training all invite a discount in the reader's
head. Consulting doesn't.

**Where the multiplier lives, now that Option 5 is the headline.** Option 5 opens with
"Advanced", which is an adjective anyone can type. So the evidence has to arrive
immediately after it, and it does: product managers who went from writing specs to
shipping the product. Worth knowing that the flattest headline puts more weight on
that second sentence and on the ledger than any of the other four would.

**On the revenue numbers.** You mentioned millions, and hundreds of millions, in
client revenue. It's off the page deliberately. Everything else in the ledger links to
somewhere it can be checked, and a revenue figure you can't attribute to a named
client sits badly next to that. If one of those product managers will let you name the
company and the number, it becomes the strongest line on the page. If not, the
specs-to-shipping sentence is the honest version of the same claim.

**The scope is deliberately wide.** Claude Code, Codex, AI in general. Anyone who
lands here from the group already knows roughly what they want, and the screener sorts
them better than a narrower headline would.

**Section 3 describes people, not services.** A list of tasks makes a reader work out
whether their task counts. A list of people lets them find themselves in one line and
stop reading. The screener's first question uses the same five, in the same order, so
the page and the form agree.

**"Rates" rather than "packages."** Packages implies a funnel. Rates is what a person
who does this work charges.

**No em dashes anywhere.** Sentence length still varies, but nothing punches for the
sake of punching. Verified: zero em dashes in the rendered page text.

**The Udemy review count is 23,929**, not 229. The v3 draft had 229, which looks like
dropped digits and would undersell the profile by two orders of magnitude.

**The testimonial comes before the pitch.** A claim lands differently once someone
else has vouched for you, so "what people say" sits above "who this is for".

**"How do you use AI today?" replaced "where are you today?"** Sorting people by
coding ability measured a skill this service doesn't require, and it made
non-technical readers feel graded before they'd booked. Depth of use is the useful
signal, and the options run from asking questions to automating real work.

**Two disqualifiers, not three.** "If you already ship with these tools daily" was
removed because it isn't true: a developer shipping every day still has everything to
gain from working in systems rather than prompts.

**The framework paragraph replaced a disclaimer.** It used to say "I'm a builder and a
teacher, not a senior systems engineer", which is the wrong thing to tell someone who
wants to learn to think like one. What's sold here is a way of working, not prompts.

**One thing worth doing after a few clients:** replace or supplement Natalia's
testimonial with two people who built something with you, plus a screenshot of what
they built. That's the only real gap left on the page.
